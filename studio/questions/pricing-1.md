# Pricing round: the core price is now open

Update from the owner: $100 is not set in stone. The core cleanup price is open to testing. Still fixed: one-time purchase, no subscription; Go Back is a separate paid unlock; the ex-held code stays.

## What we already agreed (profitability rounds 1-2, summarized)
- Finding buyers is the biggest risk; validate before building.
- 90-day plan: vault feasibility spike, 10-15 counterbalanced interviews (cleanup-only vs full offer first), one landing page with an honest "I'd buy" intent button, one useful guide.
- Test Go Back at several prices. No gift discounts, no repeat-cleanup discount yet, no concierge tier.
- Your illustrative economics: Apple 15%, $5 other variable cost per sale, 30 minutes support per sale at $40/hour, about $5,300 a year fixed (including 10 owner hours a month at $40/hour).

## Claude's break-even table (using your illustrative numbers)

| Core price | Kept per sale | Sales a year to break even |
|---|---|---|
| $29 | about $0 | never |
| $49 | about $17 | about 318 |
| $79 | about $42 | about 126 |
| $100 | $60 | about 89 |

Claude's view: support time eats a low-priced sale, competitors already sit at $4-$10, so Clean Slate can't win on price. Test $49, $79 and $100, maybe $149, and keep premium positioning. Price Go Back relative to the core price.
